# Amazon Leadership Principles Deep Dive

🎯 Interview Weight: expert - Amazon uses LP-aligned
behavioral questions exclusively; without concrete
stories mapped to specific LPs, candidates fail
every Amazon round regardless of technical skill.
The LPs also influence hiring at many other tech
companies that have adopted similar frameworks.

---

### 🎯 Model Answer

**30 seconds:**
> Amazon's Leadership Principles are the behavioral
> evaluation framework used in every loop. Each
> interviewer is assigned specific LPs and asks
> behavioral questions designed to test for them.
> Preparation means: knowing all 16 LPs by name and
> definition, having two or three strong STAR stories
> per principle, and being able to articulate the
> LP in the answer - not just tell the story. The
> most common failure is answering the question
> but not demonstrating the principle.

**3 minutes (Senior):**
> Amazon's 16 Leadership Principles function as the
> behavioral rubric for every hiring decision. Each
> interview is a structured evaluation against 2-3
> assigned LPs. The assessment is bar-raiser evaluated:
> someone not in your direct chain makes the final
> call, specifically looking for LP evidence.
>
> The principles that engineers are most frequently
> tested on: Customer Obsession (you trace technical
> decisions to customer impact), Dive Deep (you can
> go to any level of detail from architecture to
> implementation), Bias for Action (you move forward
> under uncertainty with calculated risk), Are Right
> A Lot (your judgment is calibrated and you seek
> diverse perspectives), Deliver Results (you hit
> commitments even under adverse conditions), and
> Invent and Simplify (you find simpler solutions
> to complex problems).
>
> The LP interview is not a knowledge test - it is
> an evidence test. "What is Customer Obsession?"
> is not the question. "Tell me about a time you
> made a difficult trade-off that prioritized customer
> experience over internal efficiency" is the question.
> Your story provides the evidence. The evidence is
> evaluated against the LP definition.
>
> The critical structure: your STAR story must
> demonstrate the LP behavior, not just relate to
> the LP topic. A story about a difficult customer
> situation where you advocated for the expedient
> internal solution is not a Customer Obsession story -
> it is the opposite. The LP must be in the action,
> not just in the situation.

**Framework:** WHAT -> WHY -> HOW -> TRADE-OFF -> EXAMPLE

*Adapting up:* At senior and staff level (SDE III /
Principal), the LP bar is significantly higher.
Customer Obsession is not "I fixed a customer bug" -
it is "I identified a systematic customer pain,
drove the initiative across three teams, and delivered
the fix." Dive Deep is not "I understood the code" -
it is "I identified the root cause in a system I had
never seen before by reasoning from first principles
and confirmed with data." The scale and impact of
the story must match the level.

*Adapting down:* "Amazon asks LP-based behavioral
questions exclusively. Each question is mapped to
a specific principle. Your answer must demonstrate
the principle through a concrete story - not describe
the principle or claim to have it. Story selection
is everything."

**Blank Mind Recovery:**

**(1) Restate:** "You are asking about Amazon's
Leadership Principles and how they apply in
interviews - let me walk through the key principles
engineers are assessed on and how to demonstrate them."

**(2) First principles:** "Amazon's LPs are behavioral
descriptors of how Amazonians are expected to make
decisions. The interview test is: does your historical
behavior match the LP descriptions? Your stories
are the evidence. No story = no evidence. Weak story =
weak evidence."

**(3) Bridge:** "The LP interview is like a code
review with a rubric: the rubric is the LP definitions,
your STAR stories are the code. The evaluator is
checking whether your behavior matches the rubric,
not whether you can describe the rubric."

### 📘 Concept Explanation

**What it is:**
Amazon's Leadership Principles are 16 principles
that define the expected behaviors of Amazon employees
in decision-making, people management, and technical
execution. They serve as the evaluation framework
for behavioral interviews: each interviewer tests
2-3 specific LPs using behavioral questions, and
the bar raiser evaluates for LP adherence across the
loop.

**The problem it solves:**
Large-scale hiring without a consistent evaluation
framework produces inconsistent results. The LPs
provide a shared vocabulary for evaluating candidates
that transcends technical domain and allows cross-
functional interviewers to contribute to engineering
hiring decisions.

**How it works:**

```
AMAZON LP FRAMEWORK FOR ENGINEERS
=====================================

TIER 1: MOST FREQUENTLY ASSESSED FOR ENGINEERS
  Customer Obsession: trace technical decisions
    to customer impact, advocate for customer
    even at cost to yourself
  Dive Deep: can articulate any system level,
    find root cause without incomplete information
  Bias for Action: calculated risk under uncertainty,
    do not wait for perfect information
  Deliver Results: commitments met under adverse
    conditions, do not accept "it's too hard"
  Are Right, A Lot: calibrated judgment, seek
    diverse perspectives before deciding

TIER 2: SENIOR/STAFF-SPECIFIC ASSESSMENT
  Invent and Simplify: eliminate complexity, not
    add it. If your solution is more complex than
    the problem, you have not solved it yet.
  Think Big: systemic thinking, multi-year impact
  Earn Trust: vulnerability and honesty about
    what you know and don't know
  Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit: challenge
    decisions with evidence, then commit fully

TIER 3: LEADERSHIP/MANAGER-FOCUSED
  Hire and Develop the Best: bar raiser behavior
  Insist on the Highest Standards: raise bar
  Be Earth's Best Employer (post-2021 LP)
  Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility

STORY SELECTION RULES
  One story can demonstrate multiple LPs
  Choose stories with: measurable impact,
    clear personal accountability, real trade-offs,
    and actions that are NOT what the obvious
    default would be
  Avoid: stories where you followed a playbook,
    stories where the trade-off was easy,
    stories where the LP behavior was passive
```

> **Code walkthrough:** This example demonstrates the core pattern in action. The key mechanism shows how the concept works in practice. Study the structure to understand the essential behavior and common usage.

**The key insight:**
The Amazon bar raiser is specifically looking for
"LP signals" - behaviors in your story that are NOT
what most people would do in that situation. Customer
Obsession is evidenced when you advocated for the
customer at personal cost, not when you did customer
support. The LP behavior must be visible in the
choice you made, not just the domain of the story.

**When to use it:**
Every Amazon behavioral question. Also applicable
at Google (Googleyness), Meta (Values), Microsoft
(Growth Mindset + Collaboration), and other companies
with explicit values frameworks. The preparation
methodology transfers across frameworks.

**When NOT to use it:**
Do not try to label every story with an LP in real-
time. Memorize which stories demonstrate which LPs
and pick the right story for the question asked.
Saying "This demonstrates Bias for Action" mid-story
sounds prepared to the point of being artificial.
Let the story speak; the LP is implicit.

**Alternatives:**
- Generic behavioral preparation: weaker, misses
  Amazon-specific LP signals
- Technical interview focus only: leaves the
  behavioral loop unprepared, almost always fails
- Story-per-LP approach: one story per LP, risky
  if they ask two related questions
- Story bank approach: 8-12 strong stories that
  cover multiple LPs each, more robust

**First-principles derivation:**
Amazon scales hiring at thousands of engineers per
year across hundreds of interviewers and dozens of
teams. Consistent evaluation requires a consistent
framework. The LPs provide that framework. Each LP
is operationally defined - not "be customer-focused"
but "when customers and internal efficiency conflict,
how do you decide?" This operational definition
makes behavioral questions evaluable against a
shared standard.

### 🎓 Answers by Seniority

**Junior / Mid (0-5 years):**
> At junior level (SDE I / SDE II), the LP bar for
> Customer Obsession and Deliver Results is demonstrated
> through engineering execution: you caught a bug before
> it reached the customer, you worked to meet a deadline
> without compromising quality, you proposed a simpler
> solution. The scale does not need to be large - the
> LP behavior must be clear.

*Push deeper:* "The SDE II signal for Dive Deep:
you can walk from 'the customer experienced X' through
the entire stack to 'the root cause was Y in component Z,
caused by this specific condition.' This means knowing
your system well enough to trace any observed behavior
to a root cause. Preparation: be able to give a Dive
Deep tour of any system you worked on."

---

**Senior / Staff (5+ years):**
> At SDE III and Principal level, the LP bar requires
> demonstrating the principle at larger scale and
> with higher ambiguity. Bias for Action at this
> level means driving an initiative forward when the
> path is unclear and the stakes are high, not just
> fixing a bug without waiting to be asked.

The Principal signal: you Invented and Simplified
at the system level - you identified that a complex
solution existed because the problem was being solved
the wrong way, and you designed a simpler solution
that served the same goal. Not optimizing complexity -
removing it by rethinking the problem.

*Push deeper:* "The bar raiser at Amazon looks for
two things in LP stories from senior candidates:
(1) the decision was genuinely difficult - the LP
behavior was the harder path, not the easier one;
(2) the impact was measurable - not 'the team was
better,' but 'our reliability improved by X% and
deployment frequency doubled.'"

### ⚠️ Common Misconceptions

**Misconception 1: You need a different story for
each of the 16 Leadership Principles.**

Strong story banks have 8-12 multi-use stories that
demonstrate different LP dimensions. The same project
can show Customer Obsession (you prioritized reliability
over velocity), Bias for Action (you shipped a simplification
to prove the concept), and Earn Trust (you were transparent
about the delay). Mapping stories to multiple LPs is more
efficient and provides more interview surface.

**Misconception 2: Amazon interviewers ask only one LP
per question.**

Amazon interviewers are trained to assess multiple LPs
per question. "Tell me about a conflict with a peer"
primarily assesses Earn Trust but also assesses Disagree
and Commit, Are Right A Lot, and Deliver Results.
Answer with depth that touches adjacent LPs.

---

### 🚨 Failure Modes and Diagnosis

**Failure Mode 1: Answering the LP in the title but
not demonstrating the full behavior.**

Symptom: Customer Obsession answer describes a feature
the customer wanted - but shows no sacrifice or difficult
decision. Amazon LPs include an implicit "even when it
was hard" element. Fix: ensure LP stories include a
cost: you slowed down to solve the right problem, you
pushed back on a timeline to protect quality, you told
a customer something they did not want to hear.

**Failure Mode 2: Answering "Invent and Simplify" with
a story about adding features.**

"Invent and Simplify" is explicitly about removing
complexity. A story about adding features does not
demonstrate simplification. LP mapping must be semantic,
not keyword-based. Check that your story directly
demonstrates the behavior described in the full LP
definition, not just a word in its title.

### 🎯 Interview Deep-Dive

#### Customer Obsession
- "Tell me about a time you made a technical decision
  that was harder internally but better for customers."
- "Describe a time you advocated for the customer
  when it was costly to do so."

🗣️ "**S:** We were three weeks from launch and the
PM wanted to ship a feature with a known UX issue -
it would work, but customer experience was suboptimal.
The PM argument: fix it post-launch, it won't stop
the launch.
**T:** I disagreed and needed to make the case without
being the person who delays the launch.
**A:** I analyzed the specific UX impact: the issue
would affect 30% of first-time users and cause an
average of 45 seconds of confusion before users found
the feature. I built a business case: 30% of new users
confused for 45 seconds in the first week post-launch
would show up in our onboarding funnel metrics and
be attributed to the feature itself, not the UX issue.
I proposed a targeted three-day fix scope. The PM
agreed.
**R:** The fix was delivered on time. The feature
launched with no UX issues. Onboarding completion
for this flow was 15% higher than our baseline. This
is Customer Obsession: I traced the technical issue
to customer impact and quantified it enough to change
a business decision."

#### Dive Deep
- "Tell me about a time you had to deeply understand
  a system or problem to solve it."
- "Describe a situation where you went beyond the
  surface to find a non-obvious root cause."

🗣️ "**S:** Our service had intermittent timeouts
that only occurred on Tuesdays between 9-11am.
**T:** I was the on-call engineer and it had been
open for three weeks without resolution.
**A:** I looked at what was different on Tuesday
mornings: batch jobs. Specifically, a reporting
job that ran full table scans. I traced the query
plan, found it was not using an index it should
have been, and found the index had been disabled
by a migration script six weeks earlier. The
Tuesday 9am traffic spike + the disabled index
produced a query duration spike that caused
downstream timeouts.
**R:** Re-enabling the index took 10 minutes. The
timeouts stopped immediately. The root cause was
a migration script from six weeks ago in a system
I had not written. Dive Deep means going wherever
the evidence leads."

#### Bias for Action
- "Tell me about a time you took action without
  having all the information you needed."
- "Describe a situation where waiting for full
  clarity would have been the wrong decision."

🗣️ "**S:** A production degradation was affecting
15% of requests and the root cause was unclear.
**T:** We had been investigating for two hours.
I had two hypotheses but no conclusive evidence.
**A:** I chose to act on the stronger hypothesis
without waiting for full confirmation: I implemented
a targeted mitigation (adding a circuit breaker to
the suspected dependency). The action was reversible
in under five minutes if wrong. If right, it would
stop the degradation while I finished the root cause
analysis.
**R:** The mitigation worked immediately. Root cause
confirmed 45 minutes later. Bias for Action means
taking the reversible action under uncertainty rather
than waiting. If it had been irreversible, I would
have waited for more evidence."

#### Deliver Results
- "Tell me about a time you delivered despite
  significant obstacles."
- "Describe a project where you had to change
  your approach significantly but still meet
  your commitment."

🗣️ "**S:** A critical library dependency for our
project was deprecated unexpectedly two weeks before
our delivery date.
**T:** I was the tech lead. The replacement library
had a different API. Rewriting the integration would
take, by my estimate, three to four weeks.
**A:** I analyzed the exact surface area of the
integration - it was smaller than I feared. I wrote
an adapter layer that mapped our existing calls to
the new API, which required minimal changes to our
application code. This took four days, not three
weeks. I ran it in parallel with our existing tests.
**R:** We delivered on time. The adapter was
maintainable and is still in use. Deliver Results
is not about working longer hours - it is about
finding the path to the outcome when the obvious
path is blocked."

#### Are Right A Lot
- "Tell me about a time your judgment was challenged
  and you were right. Also tell me about a time you
  were wrong."
- "How do you make decisions when you have
  incomplete information and smart people disagree
  with you?"

🗣️ "Are Right A Lot has two sides. The 'right' side:
I advocated for a microservices split that the team
was skeptical of. I made a written case with evidence:
current deployment coupling was causing 60% of our
planned releases to require full-system deployments.
The case was accepted, we split the service, and
deployment independence increased to 90%.
The 'wrong' side: I estimated that migrating our
caching layer would take two weeks. It took five.
I had not accounted for the test suite rewrite. When
I discovered the error at week two, I immediately
communicated it and revised the estimate. Are Right
A Lot includes intellectual honesty about the 'wrong'
cases - calibrated judgment means knowing your
error rate."

#### Invent and Simplify
- "Tell me about a time you found a simpler solution
  to a complex problem."
- "Describe a situation where you eliminated
  unnecessary complexity."

🗣️ "**S:** Our team had a complex microservice
orchestration layer that had been built incrementally
over two years. It had 12 services for what was
essentially one business workflow.
**T:** The complexity was causing operational issues:
distributed tracing was difficult, debugging cross-
service failures was expensive, and the team was
afraid to make changes.
**A:** I spent a week analyzing the actual data flows.
Eight of the twelve services had no shared state and
no scaling requirements - they had been broken out
prematurely. I proposed consolidating them into three
services with clear domain boundaries. The consolidation
was done incrementally over four sprints.
**R:** We went from 12 services to five. Incident
resolution time dropped by 40%. Deployment complexity
dropped significantly. Invent and Simplify is about
recognizing when complexity has become its own problem
and having the discipline to remove it."

#### Backbone and Disagree-and-Commit
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your
  manager or a decision made above you. What did
  you do?"
- "Describe a time you committed to a decision you
  disagreed with. How did you handle it?"

🗣️ "**S:** My VP decided to cut the performance
testing sprint from our release process to accelerate
delivery.
**T:** I believed this was creating a real production
risk based on our system's recent behavior under load.
**A:** I wrote a one-page risk assessment: the specific
performance degradation we had seen in the previous
three releases, the estimated probability of a
production incident without the performance sprint,
and the business cost of a production incident
post-launch. I requested 15 minutes to present it.
The VP reviewed it and maintained the decision,
accepting the risk as documented.
**R:** I committed fully - no passive resistance,
no hedging in my team communications. I documented
the decision and the risk acceptance. Three months
later, we did have a performance incident. In the
retro, the VP said the risk assessment was right
and reinstated the performance sprint. Disagree
and Commit means challenging with evidence and
accepting the outcome. The documentation created
the possibility of a later correction."

| Interviewer Type | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Technical Panel (Dive Deep) | Trace technical decision to root cause, not just architecture |
| Hiring Manager (Deliver Results) | Show you met commitments under real adversity |
| Bar Raiser (multi-LP) | Show LP behavior in the choice you made, not just the domain |
| Product PM (Customer Obsession) | Show you quantified customer impact |

### ⚖️ Comparison

| LP | What It Tests | Easy-to-Confuse Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Customer Obsession | Advocacy at personal cost | Doing customer support (low bar) |
| Dive Deep | Root cause beyond surface | Knowing your own code well |
| Bias for Action | Reversible risk under uncertainty | Reckless action without analysis |
| Deliver Results | Commitments under adversity | Working long hours (insufficient) |
| Are Right A Lot | Calibrated judgment + intellectual honesty | Being right without acknowledging when wrong |
| Invent and Simplify | Eliminating complexity | Optimizing existing complex solutions |
| Disagree and Commit | Evidence-based challenge + full commitment | Disagreeing and not committing (the failure mode) |

**The deciding factor:**
The LP bar is evidenced by the choice you made in
the story, not the domain. Customer Obsession is
shown when you chose the harder path for the customer.
Bias for Action is shown when you acted before you
were certain. Invent and Simplify is shown when you
removed the complexity rather than optimizing it.

### 🔥 Field Q&A

#### Behavioral Scenarios

Q: How do you prepare for an Amazon LP interview?

A: "Story banking: I write 8-12 STAR stories that
each cover multiple LPs. For each story I note: which
LPs it demonstrates, the measurable result, and the
specific choice I made that reflects the LP behavior.
I practice the stories aloud, not just in my head,
because the verbal delivery often reveals gaps in the
story structure. I also prepare for the 'tell me
more' question on each story: interviewers dig into
the most interesting detail, and I need to be able to
go two levels deeper on any element of any story."

Q: Tell me about an LP you have found consistently
difficult to demonstrate.

A: "Think Big has been difficult for me historically
because my career has been primarily execution-focused.
The stories that demonstrate Think Big are initiative-
level: you identified a multi-year strategic direction,
made the case, and drove it forward. I have begun
specifically looking for opportunities to contribute
to longer-horizon thinking - joining tech strategy
sessions, writing a year-ahead technical roadmap for
my team - specifically to build the story bank for
this LP."

Q: Describe a time an LP was used to evaluate you
and you felt you did not demonstrate it well.

A: "In a past loop, I was asked about Dive Deep and
gave a story that was technically accurate but not
deep enough. I described diagnosing a problem at
the service level when the interviewer wanted to
see me go to the OS or network level. I realized
this when the interviewer asked three 'tell me more'
questions and I could not go deeper. The lesson: Dive
Deep means going further than you think is necessary.
Prepare to go 2-3 levels deeper on every diagnosis story."

Q: Tell me about a time you had to demonstrate
Customer Obsession when the team resisted.

A: "We had a known performance regression in a non-
critical API that customers had mentioned once in
support tickets. The team was not prioritizing it.
I analyzed the support data more carefully: one
mention meant 10x non-reporters. I built a customer
impact calculation from our session data showing
that 8% of all API calls were affected and each
affected call added 600ms to user experience.
I presented this at sprint planning. The regression
was prioritized and fixed in two days. Customer
Obsession was not about liking customers - it was
about doing the analysis that made the customer
impact undeniable."

#### Candidate Mistakes

Q: What is the most common LP interview failure?

**What NOT to say:** Tell a story where you followed
standard practice or where the LP behavior was the
obvious path.

**Say instead:** Choose a story where the LP behavior
was the harder or less obvious choice. Customer
Obsession is evidenced when you advocated for the
customer at cost. Bias for Action is evidenced when
action before certainty was the harder path in that
context.

Q: What do candidates get wrong about Dive Deep?

**What NOT to say:** "I'm very detail-oriented.
I always understand my code well."

**Say instead:** Give a specific story: "The root cause
was at [specific technical level]. I traced it from
[observed symptom] through [specific path] to [root
cause]. The non-obvious element was [specific insight]."
The deeper you can go, the stronger the signal.

Q: How do candidates fail at Bias for Action?

**What NOT to say:** "I just do things instead of
talking about them. I don't wait."

**Say instead:** Give a story: "In [situation], waiting
for certainty would have been the safer personal choice
but the wrong call for [reason]. I acted on hypothesis
X with these safeguards: [reversibility, limited blast
radius]. The action was [result]."

Q: What is the Deliver Results trap?

**What NOT to say:** "I always deliver what I commit to."

**Say instead:** Give a specific story: "The commitment
was at risk because [specific adversity]. The obvious
path was to ask for a deadline extension. I chose to
[specific alternative action] instead. The result was
[delivered on the original commitment]."

Q: How do candidates confuse Invent and Simplify
with cleverness?

**What NOT to say:** "I came up with a clever solution
using [complex technology]."

**Say instead:** "The existing solution was complex.
I simplified it by [specific approach]. The simpler
solution achieved [same or better outcome] with [less
complexity, fewer components, lower maintenance cost]."

Q: What is the Disagree and Commit failure mode in
interviews?

**What NOT to say:** "I disagreed but I went along
with the decision."

**Say instead:** "I disagreed, made the case explicitly
with [evidence], accepted the outcome, and committed
fully - no hedged execution. Here is what full
commitment looked like: [specific behavior]."

#### Questions to Ask the Interviewer

Q: "How does this team demonstrate Customer Obsession
in technical decisions? Can you give a recent example?"

*Why:* Reveals whether Customer Obsession is real
culture or stated values.
*If asked back:* "I try to trace every technical
decision to a customer impact. If I cannot find the
customer connection, I question whether the work
should be done."

Q: "How does the bar raiser process work here?
Who serves as bar raiser and what are they looking for?"

*Why:* Reveals the actual LP evaluation rigor and
who has influence over the hiring decision.
*If asked back:* "I understand bar raisers look for
LP signals across the full loop. I try to give stories
that demonstrate the LP behavior through the choice
I made, not just the domain of the story."

Q: "What LPs does this team explicitly focus on
in its day-to-day culture?"

*Why:* Reveals which LPs are aspirational and which
are actually practiced.
*If asked back:* "I find Invent and Simplify and
Dive Deep most personally resonant. I actively look
for opportunities to remove complexity and to go
deeper on root cause analysis than the obvious level."

Q: "Can you give an example of a team decision that
demonstrates Disagree and Commit in action?"

*Why:* Reveals whether the team actually practices
this LP or just expects it from new hires.
*If asked back:* "I've both disagreed-and-committed
and been the decision-maker who someone else disagreed
with. Both sides of the LP require specific behaviors."

Q: "What is the most common LP development area
for engineers at this level on this team?"

*Why:* Reveals growth expectations and what the team
is working on as a culture.
*If asked back:* "I actively track my LP story bank
and try to identify gaps - LPs where my stories are
weak or absent. I then look for opportunities to
practice that LP behavior in my current work."

Q: "How does the team handle a situation where
Deliver Results and Do Right by Employees conflict?"

*Why:* Tests whether the team has thought through
LP tensions, which are real and important.
*If asked back:* "LPs are not ranked and they
sometimes conflict. When they do, I try to find
the path that honors both rather than optimizing
for one at the expense of the other. If forced to
choose, I think about which principle the organization
has implicitly committed to honoring in this type
of situation."
